# New video: from one line to an approved sample

One video = one aesthetic ([config/aesthetics.json](../../../config/aesthetics.json), ranked in [docs/research-aesthetics.md](../../../docs/research-aesthetics.md)) + one brief (`scenes/<scene>/brief.json`). The key art is one AI image; everything that moves is code (the Treetop recipe). No hand-written JavaScript: the scene is data (`scenes/<scene>/scene.json`, read by [src/library/autoscene.js](../../../src/library/autoscene.js)).

## Autopilot (the default, user 2026-10-21)

Truman wants to say one line, "generate me a new youtube video sample for <idea>" (a Minecraft style, pixel 8 bit, a real-life farmscape), and get back a finished, polished sample he can OK before the 10 h render, with the agent iterating by itself until then. So the flow has **one stop**: his OK on a 5 minute sample with music. The key art is picked by the vision model (the sheet is in the report so he can redirect), and the music is curated for the look and mixed in, so his OK covers the look and the sound together (the sound-first rule: the sample carries the exact final music settings; anything about the sound that changes after his OK goes back to him as a clip).

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python tools\autopilot.py run "<idea>" [--budget 6]     # the whole path; re-run the same line to resume
.venv\Scripts\python tools\autopilot.py run --scene <scene> --from <stage>   # after a fix
.venv\Scripts\python tools\autopilot.py status <scene>                 # stages, notes, spend
.venv\Scripts\python tools\autopilot.py look "<idea>"                  # only: which look fits (free to read, ~$0.02)
```

Stages (state and logs in `out/<scene>/auto/`: `state.json`, `progress.md`, one `<stage>.log` each): look, plan, keyart, pick, prep, analyze, clean, **features** (tools/plate_features.py: birds on wires, a moth at a lamp, chimney smoke, petals, a drink in an empty cup, more critter paths, the ant only when it is the signature), life, **setup** (his defaults from [config/defaults.json](../../../config/defaults.json): the 20 minute loop period, event rates never sparser than the ramen alley, the opening line from the brief's `intro_lines`, the music settings, a sound bed that matches the weather), **checks** (GATE: tools/outdoor_check.py --repair, the vision model on every tile of the weather, cutting what is inside; the local model screens first, free, and Azure confirms what it flags and has the final look at every tile, L122; then review.py lint, leaks, dry pockets, timeline), music (tools/music_auto.py when the look has none), render (5 minutes from the video's start, with the opening line and music), **review** (GATE: render QA, static check, event sheets), **critic** (GATE: tools/critic.py, the vision model's eye checks next to approved references), report (`out/<scene>/auto/sample.md`, the video in `out/samples/<scene>/`).

When it stops:
- **exit 4, a question for Truman (user 2026-10-25, L155):** his line named no weather, and rain is not the default (not every video needs rain, so check with him first). The plan stage stops before any key art is made. Ask him with ask_user (the look's usual weather, a clear or still night, snow, fog), then resume `run --scene <scene> --weather "<his answer>"`. A line that names the weather ("a rainy ramen alley", "a snowy cabin", "a misty lake") goes straight through; the brief records `weather` and `weather_why`.
- **The review gate repairs one class itself:** when the static check finds foliage or cloth still that the analysis missed (a tree, a draped blanket), autopilot traces it with `tools/hand_region.py --from-static`, gives it a sway that fits, and renders again (at most twice a run). Anything else stops for you.
- **exit 2, a gate:** read the stage's log and the evidence it names. Fix the cause in the shared code or the scene data and fix its whole class (every vessel, every near object, every path; L56). Re-run with `--from checks` (scene data or masks) or `--from render` (after a render-side change). If you are sure the critic is wrong, look at its evidence yourself, write why in `progress.md` and resume `--from report`. Keep going until a full pass is clean (L51).
- **exit 1, a failure:** the stage's log has the error; fix and `--from <stage>`. When plan says the look "is an idea that needs new code first", the idea matched a look in [docs/IDEAS.md](../../../docs/IDEAS.md) that isn't built yet (critter town): tell Truman what it needs and stop. Don't swap in another look on your own.
- **exit 3, the budget** (config/defaults.json `budget_usd_per_sample`; `--budget` overrides) **or the daily Azure cap** (config/azure.json `daily_cap_usd`, the line starting `daily cap:`): stop and ask Truman. Nothing was sent past either, and no gate failed; resume with the `--from <stage>` it names.
- **sample ready:** do your own eye pass before telling him: every critic UNSURE item (tiny birds, the moth), the first 15 s at 1:1 (the opening line), the first appearance of each event type (`sample.md` lists them), the key-art sheet. Then report in his style with the full path of the sample and of `sample.md`, and stop.

After his OK: `critic.py <scene> --render <sample> --approve --note "<his words>"` (his OK becomes approved references for the next scene of the look: autopilot's critic stage passes `--refs` from the newest approved scene of the same look, else the ramen alley's), then `tools/finish_video.py <scene>` (the 20 minute loop window, the 10 h with music, thumbnails, title and description in his form, the upload folder). When he asks for changes instead: each one becomes a lessons row the same round (and a critic check or a default when it is general), then fix and re-run from the right stage.

The manual stages below are what autopilot runs. Use them to understand and fix a gate, or when Truman asks to go step by step (then the old stops apply: key-art pick, sample, music clips).

**His own recording instead of key art** (the aquarium, 2026-10-22): no autopilot. `tools/footage_loop.py` takes a locked-off recording to 10 h: `cut` (his review cut from footage.json `in`, the final sound, no loop), `analyze` (one decode of the part after `in`), `seam` (the loop with the fewest fish ghosts and the busiest opening: look at seam_dissolve.jpg, the biggest fish one fish mid-dissolve, and opening_strips.jpg, then pin `loop_start` and `opening` in scenes/<scene>/footage.json), `heroes` (4K stills for thumbnails), `window`, `master` (the water from `tools/water_bed.py`, music planned over 10 h, QA). His colors: `tools/color_edit.py <scene>` opens an editor in the browser with iPhone's sliders, previewed live on the GPU through the render's own LUT; Save look writes footage.json `grade`, which reaches the 10 h only through a new window --tag and master. Ask first about anything the recording can't tell you: the music and its license (Mojang's covers Minecraft content only), the ambience level, the length. Lessons L123 to L134 hold what that video taught: the seam metric and the gourami check, a full-length peak check of the water bed, a near-silent music opener, thumbnails in many styles, a blurb that is true for footage, a review cut before the 10 h, the water he liked (Minecraft's own), a loop sized to the usable part, his own color edits.

## Before you start

- Read `docs/STATUS.md` and AGENTS.md (non-negotiables). No people in any key art (user 2026-10-16: humans read as AI; pets are fine). Franchise looks are allowed like Minecraft: named in prompts, only a secondary part of titles.
- Read [references/retrospective.md](references/retrospective.md) (how past videos went wrong, his standing asks, the defaults he settled on, the first-render checklist in section 7) and [references/lessons.md](references/lessons.md) before the first render. The goal is approval in one round.
- Spend so far: `.venv\Scripts\python tools\new_video.py spend --since <date>`. A video costs about $0.5-1.5 in Azure (key art ~$0.15 per image, analysis ~$0.05, verify and reviews ~$0.02 each).
- Pick what to make: rotate aesthetics so each gets tested (the research's ranked shortlist first), avoid two of the same family in a row, and run `new_video.py catalog`: a new brief must not be a near-duplicate (similarity >= 0.55 is too close; YouTube's inauthentic-content policy).

## The standing quality bar (user 2026-10-16, applies to every video)

He shouldn't have to repeat this for any video. Nothing that moves in real life may sit frozen in the picture, and the scene has to feel alive:
- **Painted transients go:** steam or smoke plumes, flames, water drops hanging from edges and the thin streams under them are removed from the key art (`new_video.py clean`: Azure image edit on a crop, color-fitted, only the masked area changes) and the code draws the moving version. Never animate on top of a painted one.
- **Rain lands:** if it rains and wet ground or water is visible, drops ring on it with perspective (plate.js `ground`); rivers and canals flow (plate.js `river` regions); drops swell and fall along every wet edge (drips.js).
- **Life in the background:** birds cross open sky (more often in rain) and sit on wires and poles where there are any (perch.js), small creatures crawl somewhere visible and come and go behind things (critters.js; one on a step and two on the ramen street), an ant crosses a table or counter if there is one (ant.js), windows dim as someone passes inside, mist drifts through the far view, plants, trees, cloth and hanging lanterns move in the gusts, and whatever hangs from them (drops, runoff) moves with them. In a storm, lightning and thunder come often (soft flashes). Truman's density for the ramen alley (2026-10-19: everything a bit more frequent): something happens every few seconds; the per-hour rates are in DECISIONS 2026-10-19.
- **Whatever the plate offers gets used (user 2026-10-19, ramen v7):** a blossoming tree sheds petals (petals.js), a canal or river carries boats most of the time (boat.js `ambient`), a chimney smokes (smoke.js; no painted chimney: a stove pipe or a roof ridge it could hide behind), a lamp in the dark draws a moth (moth.js). Look for these in every new plate.
- **The centerpiece gets the most care:** a steaming bowl or pot steams from its whole surface, from a fluid simulation (tools/steam_sim.py, run by `new_video.py life`): a soft, translucent haze over the whole broth that rises, curls a little and fades out within about a bowl width. Subtle, a hint of warmth: never a cloud or a sheet, never a few points or a box, no visible top edge, never rolling back down (user 2026-10-19: still too wispy on v7, then far too heavy and not subtle on v8, with the box's outline showing and steam rolling back down). A cup or glass the key art painted empty gets a drink (liquid.js) and, if hot, steams like the bowl: the same simulation scaled to the cup, a faint haze from its whole surface that shows from about 1 cm above the drink, so the drink stays visible (user 2026-10-19, round 4: the bowl's steam was right, the cup's two wisps read as two separate sources). Nothing drips onto the pet or the food, nor where its fall would end at their outline (plate_prep `keep_off_dry`: a vessel's contents count as food).
- **Gaps take the weather (user 2026-10-16 Treetop railing, again 2026-10-19 ramen stool):** rain falls and rings the ground between a stool's legs, a railing's balusters, a chair's rungs. Never cut a box around a near object (`not_outdoor` rectangles): trace it (SAM) and plate_prep `gap_fill` continues the view and the wet ground behind it. The object's own far parts seen through its gaps (a stool's back legs and rungs) stay dry: in a gap below the horizon, anything nearer than the wet ground of its row is cut (`gap_parts.png`; ramen v8 rained over the stool's back legs). `weather_audit.py` must report no dry pockets (it exits 1 and writes a crop of each to `out/plates/<scene>/audit_pockets/`); a real dry spot (under an eave) gets a line in the hand.json note. The other way too (user 2026-10-21, the pixel greenhouse's door, a request he had made several times): no rain over a window's frame, mullions, a door's stiles or its handle. plate_prep's frame cut keeps them dry (each pane splits its own depth; thin things nearer than their surroundings and unlike the view's colors are structure) and weather_audit fails on wet frames (crops in `audit_frames/`).
- **Every revision is remembered (user 2026-10-19):** each change Truman ever asked for is a row in [references/lessons.md](references/lessons.md), with how it is built in and how it is checked. `tools/review.py <scene> [--render <dir>]` runs every automatic check and lists the eye checks; it must PASS and you do the eye checks before he sees anything. When he asks for something new, add a row in the same round.
- **A signature detail (user 2026-10-19, on the ramen ant: silly and unique, something people comment on):** every brief names one small, odd, living thing only this video has (`signature_detail`): an ant crossing the counter, a mouse peeking out, a frog on a step, a ladybug on a leaf. Keep the details he liked (petals, neon flicker, the moth, the ant) wherever the plate allows. The scene must really have it: plate_features gives a critter its check drops one re-trace with the reason, review.py's L112 lint fails while the signature has no layer, and then it is placed by hand on the surface's measured top face (SAM's outline, its centerline; L83, L112). Each creature walks at its own pace (a snail 0.04 body lengths a second, insects and a gecko about 0.3).
- **Weather sits where it would** (user 2026-10-18): rain only in the open, rings only on level ground, drops only along real edges, and every streak and splash upright. Drips splash where they land. A flickering light's reflection on wet ground flickers with it. Check `weather_audit.py`, `refl_preview.jpg` and the stills yourself. Since the Minecraft tea house (2026-10-22, L113 to L116): a drop hangs only under something nearer than the view below it, never from a lantern under the roof; hand and outdoor_check cuts always stick (a cover's holes count as openings only where they are clearly farther than the cover); an opening's box stops at the frame edge when the model's box comes within 4% of it; water seen "through a railing" must follow the water's own plane; and outdoor ground the analysis missed gets its rain and rings by hand (`outdoor` and `water` in hand.json) unless the outdoor check judges it sheltered: its judgment stands, and a surface is wet or dry all along, never in part (the tea house's paving). Open view that an analysis cover took in (the Burdock Shelter's leaf, traced with the forest above it: the rain stopped at a straight vertical line) goes in hand.json `outdoor_open`, which is laid over the analysis's covers; a plain `outdoor` polygon stays under them (L146). Trace it as the cover minus the object itself, grown a few px into the view already wet, so no dry seam is left where the two meet. A runoff stream always pours from a real edge, never out of open view (review L149; eave_runoff trusts only a cover clearly nearer than the view), and a leaf or cloth held out in the rain drips along its lower edge when its hand region says `"drips": true`, except where a drop would land on a character or what it holds (L149).
- **Nothing inside under the weather (user 2026-10-21, the pixel greenhouse v6: rain still fell on the plants by the lamp after earlier fixes, and he wants it fixed for good):** no rain over a plant, a vine, a pot, a lamp, a frame or beam, and no rings on indoor water, whatever made the mask (depth, the analysis, a hand polygon, a SAM pane). `tools/outdoor_check.py <scene> --repair` shows the vision model every tile of where the weather draws at 1:1, tinted, each patch numbered, and cuts what it finds inside (SAM, kept to the thing's own colors, a cell around it; hand.json `outdoor_cuts`). On pixel art plate_prep also keeps weather strips under 3 cells wide dry: rain in a sliver beside a lamp or a post reads as rain running down it, even when the sliver is real glass. Autopilot's checks stage runs it, review.py fails without its pass, and you look at `out/plates/<plate>/outdoor_check/outdoor_check.jpg` and every tile yourself after any mask change, patch by patch (L100; the model passed the sliver by the greenhouse's lantern). With `--repair` it screens every tile on the local model first (Qwen3.5-9B through llama.cpp, free) and only cuts what Azure confirms, then Azure looks at every tile last (user 2026-10-22: the local model for first passes to save cost, Azure for the final check; L122, config/local_vision.json): keep the GPU free while it runs.
- **And the other way: rain in front of everything out in the open (2026-10-22, the Minecraft tea house, L121):** a building, roof, wall, lantern, steps or tree across a garden, a pond or a street is part of the view and takes the rain; only the room or veranda the camera stands in, and what is under its roof, stay dry. The tea house shipped with the rain cut in front of its far pavilion and other buildings: the outdoor check read them as "solid parts of the building", and every later check took its cuts as right (weather_audit counts them as dry on purpose). Since 2026-10-25 outdoor_check's prompt says so and the pavilion tile is in its selftest (4 of 4 must pass). On every render look at `<render>/review/rain_motion.jpg` (tools/rain_motion.py, made by review.py: where the rain moves over a dozen frames): no dark hole in the shape of anything out in the view.
- **What the code adds matches the plate's focus:** the ant on an out-of-focus foreground is blurred like it (ant.js reads `defocus.png`), and is better placed where the plate is sharp. The snail (critters.js) doesn't read it yet: keep its path on in-focus ground.
- **The pet only breathes (user 2026-10-18):** a pet painted into the key art gets breathing scaled to its size and an occasional sigh. Nothing more: no ear flicks or tail twitches in new videos. Generated video of it (lifting its head, looking around, sitting up) looked eerie, jerky and obviously AI on the ramen alley and is never used, in any style. Paint the pet asleep or resting. A pet that really moves must be a fully rendered 3D model or a library asset (rigged model, sprite sheet) animated in code, only in looks it matches (blocky, pixel art, low-poly), and free (CC0; nothing paid) (DECISIONS 2026-10-18).
- **Fire is live:** painted flames are removed from the plate, and the fire plays from short clips of the key art's own fire animated by the local video model (`new_video.py life`: tools/fire_clips.py, fire.js walks through them with soft joins). Code-drawn flames are only the fallback. The whole fire moves, its bright center most of all (user 2026-10-25, the Burdock Shelter: the flames looked good, but the fire's center didn't move): a small fire is cropped tighter and upscaled for the model, and a clip whose center holds still under moving licks is dropped (core flow under 1.6, L153); look at `out/plates/<scene>/fire/center_strip.jpg` (the center's flames change shape every quarter second).
- **Rain shows and ends where the picture does (user 2026-10-25, the Burdock Shelter v10):** in front of a dark night forest a physical drop is as dark as the forest, so the rain layer's `minContrast` lifts drops there (review fails when over 10% of the open rain is dark and faint, L152). The rain never stops at a straight line the painting doesn't have, like the side of the analysis's opening box: the ground under a shelter is traced (SAM), never boxed, and the rain hits the ground in front of it (review lint L151, tools/mask_edges.py).
- **Check before showing:** `tools\motion_review.py <preview.mp4> --static <scene>` must pass (exit 0): no region of a moving kind still, and nothing the vision model calls still may measure still inside its own SAM mask (rain or drips falling in front don't count as motion). When it flags a thing the analysis missed (cloth, leaves), add it as a region in `assets/keyart/<scene>.hand.json` (SAM masks from boxes, as for the ramen noren). Look at crops yourself too.

## Procedure

1. **Brief:** `.venv\Scripts\python tools\new_video.py plan <scene> --aesthetic <id> --auto [--hint "..."]` writes `scenes/<scene>/brief.json` (the model writes setting, pet, props, foreground/midground/background, light, signature motion and a title idea, avoiding every existing brief) and `keyart.json`. Read it, edit the brief if needed, then `new_video.py prompt <scene>`.
   - The prompt boilerplate already asks for no painted steam, smoke or hanging drops (they are animated later); some still slip in, and `clean` removes them.
   - Good briefs: one locked-off wide shot; a sleeping animal with ears and tail visible; 1-2 props the code animates (steaming cup, fire, candles, a hanging lantern or chime, string lights, neon); a window or opening showing weather; **clear glass** (the code adds the rain on it).
2. **Key art:** `new_video.py keyart <scene> --n 2` (3-4 for a new aesthetic). Look at `out/<scene>/keyart_sheet.jpg` yourself. `new_video.py pick <scene> --auto` scores the candidates (composition, animatable elements, artifacts, brief).
   - **STOP 1:** show the user the sheet and the auto pick; wait for their pick, then `new_video.py pick <scene> <png>`.
3. **Plate:** `new_video.py prep <scene>` (4K upscale, depth, lights).
4. **Analysis:** `new_video.py analyze <scene>` runs [tools/analyze_plate.py](../../../tools/analyze_plate.py) (`--verify` adds the model's check of the overlay; `--reuse` skips the model call). It writes:
   - `assets/keyart/<scene>.masks.json`: regions (glass, fire, steam, candles, floating candles one by one, lanterns, neon, string lights, pet with its look/pose/place, plants, curtains, water with its flow direction, clouds, dust, fireflies), outdoor/sky/water polygons (water = rivers, canals and the wet ground rain lands on), the near objects cut out of the outdoors, cleanup of painted bolts, rain streaks and snowflakes, and the pixel grid for pixel art. **Re-running overwrites it.**
   - `assets/keyart/<scene>.clean.json`: the painted transients to remove (steam plumes, flames, drops along the wet edges and under outdoor cloth). Edit freely; not rewritten once the plate is cleaned.
   - `scenes/<scene>/scene.json` (a draft: layers incl. drips, birds, critter, fire; events incl. birds, critter, windows; mist, ground rain; sound bed; only rewritten with `--force-scene`) and a 3-line `scene.js`.
   - Hand fixes go in `assets/keyart/<scene>.hand.json` (merged by plate_prep, never overwritten): regions traced with SAM from a box (a near object the analysis missed, with `"near": true, "dry": true` so the weather stays off it; never a `not_outdoor` rectangle around it: the street seen between its legs loses its rain, the ramen stool 2026-10-19), `inpaint` polygons, `inpaint_bright` boxes.
5. **Clean:** `new_video.py clean <scene>` runs [tools/plate_clean.py](../../../tools/plate_clean.py) (`--dry` first writes `out/plates/<scene>/clean_masks.jpg`; ~$0.06 per edit window) and rebuilds the plate from `assets/keyart/<scene>/clean.png` (committed, with `clean.json` provenance). Look at `out/plates/<scene>/clean_preview.jpg`: nothing painted that moves may remain, and the repaint must match the style. Re-run one item with `plate_clean.py <scene> --only <name>`.
6. **Life:** `new_video.py life <scene>`:
   - the critter sprite ([tools/critter.py](../../../tools/critter.py): gpt-image with a transparent background, in the scene's style; ~$0.07);
   - no pet clips: a painted pet only breathes (the draft's `events.pet` gives it sighs; breathing is automatic). `tools/pet_clips.py` made generated behavior clips of the painted pet and is retired for that (DECISIONS 2026-10-18); it stays in the repo because fire_clips.py uses its model loader. Never add a `pet` layer or `events.petAct` to a scene.
   - the fire's clips ([tools/fire_clips.py](../../../tools/fire_clips.py) `auto`): Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B (Apache-2.0, local GPU) animates a crop of the ORIGINAL key art's fire (2 clips of 4 s, ~10 min each); `loop` color-fits them to the plate, cuts them to the fire's mask and finds the frames each could jump to. Check `out/plates/<scene>/fire/sheet.jpg`. Generation stops and retries when a step takes over 45 s (the GPU ran out of memory: close video players and renders while it runs).
   - the steam over every cup, bowl or pot with a measured opening ([tools/steam_sim.py](../../../tools/steam_sim.py)): a 2D fluid simulation from the fitted opening, 120 s at 30 fps (~2-3 min of GPU each), a looping flipbook in `out/plates/<scene>/steam/<region>/`; skipped when up to date (same opening, loop length and tool version; `--force` after changing the steam layer's `lean`). It is scaled to the vessel: its real size comes from the region's `size_cm` or its name (a cup 8 cm, a bowl 20, a pot 26), the grid keeps the same detail per half-width, and a smaller vessel's steam evolves faster (`tempo`, the square root of the size ratio: buoyant plumes scale that way). A drink's steam shows from `onset_cm` (1) above it, soup's right at the surface (0). The fluid runs in a box far bigger than the plume and only the middle is saved: a box the plume's size acts as a lid, and the steam pools under it into a sheet with an edge and rolls down the sides (ramen v8). steam.json records `edge_max` (steam left at the saved box's edges, must be ~0) and `down_max` (steam moving down); review.py fails a plume over 0.01 or 0.06. How strong it looks: the steam layer's `simOpacity` (0.5) or a vessel's own `opacity` in `per` (the ramen cup 0.7: a small plume over a bright street fades away at 0.5), `simCap` (0.4, the most it ever covers), and the tool's `fade_from`/`fade_to` (where it fades out, in half-widths above the far rim). `--preview 20` writes a quick look; `--set k=v,...` tunes it (DEFAULTS in the tool); `--out <dir>` for trials. Check it on black (`--params only=steam,plate=black`), at 1:1 on 6 frames 0.2 s apart, and a still every 30 s across the 120 s loop.
7. **Check the analysis yourself** (look before you show):
   - `out/plates/<scene>/regions_preview.png`: every region labeled, boxes tight, nothing important missing.
   - `.venv\Scripts\python tools\leak_check.py <scene>` must PASS: no weather drawn over pets, cups, candles, lanterns, fires or indoor plants (overlay: `out/<scene>/leak_check.png`, outdoors in magenta).
   - `.venv\Scripts\python tools\weather_audit.py <scene>`: every weather source over the plate in `out/plates/<scene>/weather_audit.jpg` (blue rain, green rain rings, red upright surfaces still marked wet, yellow drops along edges, cyan strand drips, magenta runoff). Rain only in the open, rings only on level ground (red must be ~0), drops only under real edges, strand drips never falling through a sign. To Truman this is the easiest tell between AI and hand-made work.
   - `.venv\Scripts\python tools\outdoor_check.py <scene> --repair`: the vision model on every tile of where the weather draws (must PASS; cached per tile; the local model screens, Azure confirms and looks last). Look at `out/plates/<scene>/outdoor_check/outdoor_check.jpg` (cuts in cyan) and the `tile_*_tint.jpg` crops: magenta only over the view, never a leaf, a frame or indoor water, and every cut on something of the room itself, never on a building, lantern or tree out in the view (L121). After changing its prompt or evidence, `outdoor_check.py --selftest` must score 3 of 3.
   - Cleanup: compare `plate_raw.png` and `plate.png` crops wherever something was removed, and `clean_preview.jpg`.
8. **Tune `scene.json`** (keys in autoscene.js): event rates (`events`), layer strengths, region effects (`regions: { <name>: { flicker: {...}, bob: {...} } }`, `null` turns a region off), the sound (`audio.bed`, `audio.extra_db`). Region defaults by kind: `REGION_FX` in autoscene.js; beds: `BEDS` in tools/cozy/audio/ambience.py. Two keys every scene needs from the start:

## Known limits (check by eye)

- The model's boxes are approximate; SAM fixes most. Open views (`opening`) keep the model's box, so near structures must be `cover` elements or hand fixes.
- Depth can't separate a windowsill from the view in illustrations: near kinds and indoor plants are dry by meaning, not depth; the model's `side` answer decides the rest.
- A glass room (a greenhouse, a conservatory): the model calls every pane "inside" (never a reason to cut a pane: analyze_plate skips them), the depth reads side and roof panes as near surfaces (their view goes in hand.json `outdoor`, here picked by color), SAM's pane masks can be the plants in front (hand.json `no_view`) or leave out a barn seen through the pane (hand.json `pane_hull: true`; opt-in, it leaked sills and stone on other plates). Review lint L79 fails a pane with no outdoors behind it; plate_prep keeps drops on a pane only where it looks out.
- Pixel art: painted rain dashes are a cell wide; plate_prep `derain_cells` removes them on the grid after the rebuild. Compare the sky in plate_raw.png and plate.png at 1:1. The output pass shows each cell's center pixel, so anything thinner than a cell vanishes: autoscene sets floors for pixel looks (rain streaks a cell wide, critters ~6.5 cells, birds 6 cells and the moth drawn cell by cell, the moth a silhouette over a lamp's glass) and intro_title.py draws the pixel font on the grid. Check small things and the opening line at 1:1 from a render, never from a preview drawn off the grid.
- The image edit in `clean` can repaint a little differently from the art around it (texture, a log arrangement). The color fit hides seams; check `clean_preview.jpg` and re-run an item if it looks off.
- Painted drips far from the opening's edges are found by a zoomed vision scan (`plate_clean.py` kind `strands`), confirmed on a close-up sheet (`out/plates/<scene>/strands_verify.jpg`); a missed one can be added as a box in `clean.json`.
- Generated video of a painted pet looks eerie and obviously AI however well it is matted, color-fit and rated (the ramen alley's cat, 2026-10-18). pet.js and `events.petAct` are retired and no scene uses them; a render that does prints `[page] pet: ...`.
- Slanted rain is usually not the rain: render the layers alone (`--params only=rain`, `ground=off`, `layers=off`) and diff the stills before touching the wind. On the ramen alley it was the ground rings' splash crowns (fixed in plate.js).
- A real snail is tiny at a few meters; the critter is drawn about twice life so it reads, and no bigger: at 8 times life the ramen snail covered the riser behind it and floated (2026-10-19). Check it at 1:1 on a still where it is mid-path: its foot on the surface, nothing of the surface behind it showing under it.
- The critter must never appear or vanish in plain view. After moving its path by hand, run `analyze_plate.py <scene> --critter-occ` (every track) and check `out/plates/<scene>/occ_preview.jpg` (red = what they hide behind), then `tools/critter_ends.py <scene>`: the renderer's own rule in Python (the whole creature covered at each end, or off the frame) and every cover standing at least as near as the ground at its end, never a patch of the same ground it would melt into (L144; review.py's lint L144 runs it, since the render's `[page] critter: ... in plain sight` warning never reaches its log). A path end needs to sit next to something solid (a rock, a pot, a plant) or past the frame's edge (a path end inside the frame on open floor took a brick as its cover; on the Burdock Shelter's forest floor SAM took flat wet stone and leaf litter). Depth alone can't do this: a planter beside a step is only ~10% nearer.
- Art changes he asks for on a key art already in use (a prettier sky, other crops): `tools/art_edit.py <scene> --sky | --sam "x,y;..." | --green <polygon> --prompt "..."` (`--dry` first: the mask view is free). No color fit, logged in clean.json, then `plate_prep --keep-depth`; pin the pixel grid in hand.json first on a pixel plate (an edit moves the estimate). Look at the before and after at 1:1, and at anything thin in the sky (a windpump, a pole).
- Painted bolts and snowflakes are still removed heuristically (plate_prep `cleanup`): compare raw and cleaned crops.
